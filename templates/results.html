Why Is This Important?

Sycophancy — the tendency of AI models to tell users what they want to hear rather than what is true — is emerging as one of the most consequential alignment failures in deployed language models.

  • When models validate misconceptions instead of correcting them, users form false beliefs reinforced by perceived authority of AI
  • In medical contexts, LLMs showed up to 100% compliance with logically flawed requests, generating dangerous misinformation by prioritizing helpfulness over accuracy npj Digital Medicine, 2025
  • Sycophantic AI has been shown to decrease prosocial intentions and promote dependency in users, undermining autonomous judgment Sharma et al., 2025
  • Models affirm whichever side a user adopts in moral conflicts 48% of the time, regardless of the ethical merits ELEPHANT, 2025
  • Vulnerable users are at heightened risk: AI companions that validate rather than challenge can reinforce negative emotions and enable harmful decision-making Nature Machine Intelligence, 2025

Previous Research

Research on LLM sycophancy has accelerated rapidly since 2023. Key findings establish that sycophancy is not a single behavior but a family of distinct, independently steerable tendencies.

Sycophancy Is Multidimensional
  • Sycophantic agreement (conforming to user beliefs), sycophantic praise (excessive flattery), and social sycophancy (preserving the user's self-image) are encoded along distinct linear directions in model latent space ICLR 2026
  • Each behavior can be independently amplified or suppressed using activation steering, suggesting they arise from separate learned representations
  • Syco-Bench found weak correlations between different sycophancy tests, confirming that each test captures a relatively independent aspect Syco-Bench
Measuring Social Sycophancy
  • The ELEPHANT benchmark characterizes sycophancy as excessive preservation of a user's face (desired self-image), testing across 11 models ELEPHANT, ICLR 2026
  • LLMs preserve user face 45 percentage points more than humans even when users describe manipulation, deception, or relational harm
  • When prompted with perspectives from either side of a moral conflict, models affirm the user's position regardless of ethical merit
Domain-Specific Risks
  • Medicine: All five tested frontier LLMs showed high initial compliance with prompts that misrepresent drug relationships, prioritizing helpfulness over logical consistency npj Digital Medicine
  • Mathematics: BrokenMath found that even the best models produce sycophantic answers 29% of the time on deliberately false theorem proving tasks BrokenMath, 2025
  • Video understanding: Video-LLMs align with user input even when it directly contradicts visual evidence ViSE, 2026
Multi-Turn Dynamics
  • Models that initially provide correct answers can be talked out of their position through persistent user disagreement EMNLP 2025
  • SycEval found sycophantic behavior in 58.19% of cases across ChatGPT-4o, Claude-Sonnet, and Gemini-1.5-Pro, with regressive sycophancy (leading to incorrect answers) in 14.66% SycEval, 2025
  • Larger models often perform worse on truthfulness (inverse scaling), attributed to memorizing popular misconceptions from training data TruthfulQA
Mitigation Approaches
  • Test-time interventions and supervised fine-tuning substantially reduce but do not eliminate sycophantic behavior
  • Activation additions can independently steer agreement, praise, and face-preservation along separate latent directions
  • Prompt engineering (e.g., instructing models to prioritize accuracy) provides partial mitigation but is not robust across contexts

What This Benchmark Measures

The Agreeability Bench presents LLMs with questions containing common misconceptions and evaluates whether models correct or validate false beliefs.

  • Each response is scored on a z-score agreeability scale where 0 represents average human behavior
  • Negative scores indicate higher agreeability — the model tends to validate misconceptions to maintain rapport
  • Positive scores indicate lower agreeability — the model prioritizes factual correction over user comfort
  • Scoring is performed by Claude, which analyzes response patterns including hedging, validation language, and directness of correction
  • Results include per-model explanations citing specific behavioral evidence from the responses
